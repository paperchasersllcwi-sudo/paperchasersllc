What to Do When a Tenant Avoids Being Served in Wisconsin

By Michael Winius, Paper Chasers LLC
Disclaimer: This article is provided for educational and informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Paper Chasers LLC is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice or legal representation. Court rules and local practices change, and every case is different. Confirm current requirements with the clerk of courts, and speak with a qualified attorney about your specific situation.

Introduction

Some tenants know an eviction is coming and decide not to answer the door. Others work odd hours, have moved out without saying so, or have someone else in the unit. Whatever the reason, a tenant who avoids service does not stop the case. It does mean the attempts have to be made the right way and written down.

Make Real Attempts at Different Times

Wisconsin law expects hand delivery first. Before papers can be left with someone else or posted, the server has to show reasonable diligence (Wis. Stat. § 801.11). In practice, that usually means several attempts on different days and at different times, such as an early morning, an evening and a weekend. Three tries at 2:00 p.m. on weekdays will not look diligent to a court commissioner.

Use What You Know as the Landlord

You often know more about the tenant than anyone else. Share it with your server:

  • Work schedule or employer, if the tenant listed one on the application
  • Vehicle make, model and plate
  • A photo, if you have one on file
  • Other adults who live in the unit
  • Times you or your staff have seen the tenant coming or going

Leaving Papers With a Household Member

If the tenant can't be reached after diligent attempts, the papers can be left at the tenant's usual home with a competent family member at least 14 years old, or another adult who lives there, who is told what the papers are (§ 801.11). A neighbor or a guest does not count.

When Nobody Will Answer

Wisconsin's small claims rules provide fallback methods for eviction cases when personal and household service fail, such as posting at the property and mailing a copy (§ 799.12). These options depend on documented diligent attempts. Check with the clerk of courts in your county about how they expect it to be done and proven.

Write Down Every Attempt

The proof of service should list each attempt: date, time, address, who answered (if anyone) and what happened. Notes like "lights on, TV audible, no answer" or "vehicle in driveway" help show the tenant was there and avoiding service. If service is ever challenged, this record is what the court looks at.

Don't Wait

Every failed attempt eats into the time before your court date. Start service as soon as the clerk issues the summons. If the deadline passes, you may need a new return date, which pushes the whole case back. See The 5-Day Eviction Service Deadline.

What Not to Do

  • Don't serve the papers yourself or send a friend who has a stake in the case.
  • Don't change the locks, shut off utilities or remove belongings to force the tenant out. Self-help evictions are prohibited in Wisconsin.
  • Don't tape papers to the door and call it done without documented attempts.
