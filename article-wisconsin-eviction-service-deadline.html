The 5-Day Eviction Service Deadline in Wisconsin: How to Count It

Research Library › Landlords & Property Managers
By Michael Winius, Paper Chasers LLC
Disclaimer: This article is provided for educational and informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Paper Chasers LLC is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice or legal representation. Court rules and local practices change, and every case is different. Confirm current requirements with the clerk of courts, and speak with a qualified attorney about your specific situation.

Introduction

In a Wisconsin eviction, the summons and complaint must be served at least 5 days before the return date (Wis. Stat. § 799.05). That sounds simple, but many landlords count it wrong, and a miscounted deadline can cost you your court date.

What Is the Return Date?

The return date is the first court appearance listed on the summons. In eviction cases, it is set between 5 and 25 days after the summons is issued (§ 799.05). It is the date the deadline counts back from.

Weekends and Holidays Don't Count

Under Wisconsin's general rule for counting time, when a period is shorter than 11 days, Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays are left out (Wis. Stat. § 801.15). Because the eviction service period is 5 days, it is counted in business days. Several county sheriff's offices describe it the same way: at least five working days before the return date.

An Example

Return date
Tuesday, the 18th.
Count 5 business days
The day of service doesn't count. The 5 days are Wed 12th, Thu 13th, Fri 14th, Mon 17th and Tue 18th. The weekend of the 15th and 16th is skipped.
Serve by
Tuesday, the 11th, at the latest. Serving on the 12th or later is too late. Aim for days earlier.

A holiday in that window pushes the deadline back one more business day. When in doubt, serve earlier. Ask the clerk of courts if you are unsure how your county counts it.

Plan for Failed Attempts

The deadline is the last safe day, not the target. Tenants are not always home, and some avoid the door. Build in time for at least three or four attempts on different days. A good rule is to hand the papers to your server the same day the clerk issues the summons.

What Happens If You Miss It

If service is not completed in time, the court generally can't go forward on that return date. You may need a new summons or a new return date, and then you start the service process again. That can push your hearing back by weeks.

Don't Confuse It With the Notice Period

The 5-day notice to pay rent or vacate, given before you file, is a different deadline with its own counting rules (Wis. Stat. §§ 704.17 and 704.21). This article covers only the deadline to serve the summons and complaint after filing.
