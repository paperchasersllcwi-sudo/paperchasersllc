How to Serve Eviction Papers in Wisconsin: A Landlord's Guide

By Michael Winius, Paper Chasers LLC
Disclaimer: This article is provided for educational and informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Paper Chasers LLC is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice or legal representation. Court rules and local practices change, and every case is different. Confirm current requirements with the clerk of courts, and speak with a qualified attorney about your specific situation.

Introduction

Filing an eviction is only half the job. Before a court commissioner will hear your case, the tenant has to be properly served with the summons and complaint. If service is late, done by the wrong person, or not documented, your court date can be pushed back, and every week of delay is another week of lost rent.

This guide explains how service of eviction papers generally works in Wisconsin small claims court, with a focus on the Fox Valley counties: Outagamie, Winnebago, Calumet and Brown.

Notice First, Then Filing, Then Service

Landlords often mix up two different steps. The termination notice (for example, a 5-day notice to pay rent or vacate) is served before you file. The summons and complaint are served after you file with the clerk of courts. This article is about the second step. Notice rules are in Wis. Stat. ch. 704, and they have their own service requirements.

Who Can Serve the Summons and Complaint

Any adult resident of Wisconsin who is not a party to the case can serve papers (Wis. Stat. § 801.10). That means you cannot serve your own tenant, even if you own the property yourself. Your property manager or maintenance staff also should not serve if they have a stake in the case. Most landlords use the county sheriff or a private process server.

How Papers Can Be Served

1. Hand delivery
The papers are handed directly to the tenant named in the case (§ 801.11).
2. Left at the home
If hand delivery fails after diligent attempts, the papers can be left at the tenant's usual home with a competent family member 14 or older, or an adult who lives there (§ 801.11).
3. Eviction fallback
If both fail after diligent attempts, small claims rules allow alternatives for evictions, such as posting at the property and mailing (§ 799.12). Ask the clerk before relying on this.

Each step depends on the one before it. A server who posts papers on the door without first making real attempts at hand delivery is inviting a challenge.

The Deadline

In an eviction, the summons must be served at least 5 days before the return date, which is the first court date on the summons (§ 799.05). Because Wisconsin skips weekends and holidays when counting periods shorter than 11 days (§ 801.15), that usually means 5 business days. See The 5-Day Eviction Service Deadline for how to count it.

Proof of Service

After service, the server signs a proof of service (an affidavit or certificate) that says who was served, where, when and how. It gets filed with the court before or at the return date. If service was made at the home or by posting, the proof should also list each earlier attempt with the date, time, location and result. That record is what shows the court the server used reasonable diligence.

Common Mistakes

  • Serving the tenant yourself, or having someone with a stake in the case do it.
  • Waiting until the last few days before the court date to start trying.
  • Naming only one tenant when several adults are on the lease.
  • Posting papers on the door without documented attempts first.
  • Losing track of the proof of service, so it never reaches the court file.
