Firearm Rights Restoration Through a Wisconsin Pardon: What a Wisconsin Pardon May and May Not Accomplish

Research Library › Pardons & Clemency
By Michael Winius, Paper Chasers LLC
Disclaimer: This article is provided for educational and informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Individuals seeking legal advice concerning firearm rights restoration or executive clemency should consult a licensed attorney. Introduction

One of the most common reasons individuals pursue executive clemency in Wisconsin is the restoration of rights lost as a result of a felony conviction. Among those rights, firearm possession frequently becomes a central concern. This article examines firearm rights restoration through the Wisconsin pardon process and explains how firearm-related concerns fit within the broader clemency framework.

Constitutional Authority for Pardons

The authority to grant pardons originates directly from the Wisconsin Constitution. Article V, Section 6 provides that the Governor may grant reprieves, commutations, and pardons after conviction. The pardon power is an executive function independent of the courts.

Why Applicants Seek Firearm Rights Restoration

Many applicants identify firearm restrictions as a significant collateral consequence of a felony conviction. Common reasons include:

  • Hunting and sporting activities
  • Family traditions involving hunting
  • Employment opportunities
  • Personal protection concerns
  • General restoration of civil rights

What a Wisconsin Pardon Is

A pardon is an act of executive forgiveness. It recognizes rehabilitation and personal growth following conviction. A pardon is not a judicial proceeding and does not overturn a conviction.

What a Wisconsin Pardon Does Not Do

A pardon does not:

  • Expunge a conviction
  • Seal court records
  • Vacate a judgment of conviction
  • Declare innocence

The conviction remains part of the historical court record.

How Firearm Rights Fit Into the Clemency Process

Publicly available pardon materials suggest that applicants often identify firearm rights restoration as one component of a broader request for executive clemency. Decision makers generally examine rehabilitation, accountability, and community involvement rather than focusing exclusively upon firearms.

What the Pardon Advisory Board Appears to Evaluate

Public hearing records suggest recurring consideration of:

  • Acceptance of responsibility
  • Rehabilitation
  • Employment history
  • Educational achievements
  • Community involvement
  • Character references
  • Passage of time since conviction
  • Demonstrated need for clemency

Common Applicant Mistakes

  • Treating the application solely as a firearm rights request.
  • Providing little documentation supporting rehabilitation.
  • Failing to demonstrate accountability.
  • Submitting weak character references.
  • Focusing entirely on past circumstances rather than current accomplishments.

Historical Context

Wisconsin's pardon process was inactive for several years during the administration of Governor Scott Walker, who issued no pardons. In 2019, Governor Tony Evers restored the pardon process through Executive Order No. 30. Since restoration of the process, more than 2,000 pardons have been granted, representing the most active pardon period in modern Wisconsin history.

Practical Considerations

Applicants should understand that successful clemency applications typically present a complete picture of rehabilitation. Firearm rights restoration may be one benefit sought by an applicant, but the strongest applications generally emphasize personal growth, community contribution, accountability, and evidence of lasting change.

Conclusion

A Wisconsin pardon is not primarily about firearms. It is about demonstrating rehabilitation, responsibility, and positive contributions to society. For some applicants, restoration of firearm-related rights may be one result of a pardon. However, successful clemency applications typically present a broader narrative supported by documentation and evidence of meaningful personal growth.
