Wisconsin Clemency Application Document Checklist and Preparation Guide

By Michael Winius, Paper Chasers LLC
Disclaimer: This document is provided for educational and informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Paper Chasers LLC is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice or legal representation.

Introduction

The Wisconsin clemency application process is fundamentally a documentation and rehabilitation review process. Successful applications are typically supported by organized records, supporting documentation, and evidence of rehabilitation.

1. Basic Identification Information

Applicants should be prepared to provide:

  • Full legal name
  • Prior names or aliases
  • Date of birth
  • Current address
  • Telephone number
  • Email address

Recommended documents:

  • Wisconsin driver's license or state identification card
  • Proof of current address

2. Criminal Case Information

For each conviction, gather:

  • County of conviction
  • Case number
  • Offense(s) of conviction
  • Date of conviction
  • Sentence imposed
  • Date sentence completed

Recommended documents:

  • Judgment of Conviction
  • Criminal Complaint
  • Information or Amended Information
  • Sentencing Orders
  • Revocation records, if applicable

3. Sentence Completion Documentation

Applicants should gather records establishing completion of all sentence components, including incarceration, probation, parole, and extended supervision. Recommended documents:

  • Certificate of Discharge
  • DOC discharge records
  • Probation discharge records
  • Extended supervision discharge records

4. Employment History

Gather documentation showing stable employment and work history. Recommended documents:

  • Resume
  • Employer verification letters
  • Pay stubs
  • W-2 forms

5. Education and Training

Educational achievement may demonstrate rehabilitation and personal growth. Recommended documents:

  • GED or high school diploma
  • College transcripts
  • Trade certifications
  • Professional licenses
  • Apprenticeship records

6. Rehabilitation Documentation

Gather records reflecting treatment, counseling, and rehabilitative programming. Examples:

  • AODA treatment completion records
  • Mental health treatment records
  • Anger management completion certificates
  • Domestic violence programming records

7. Community Involvement

Community service and civic participation may strengthen an application. Recommended documentation:

  • Volunteer records
  • Letters from clergy
  • Certificates of recognition
  • Community service records

8. Character References

Strong applications often include letters from:

  • Employers
  • Business owners
  • Clergy
  • Community leaders
  • Professional colleagues

Letters should describe the applicant's character, rehabilitation, and contributions to the community.

9. Demonstrated Need for a Pardon

Applicants should identify a specific reason for seeking clemency. Examples include:

  • Employment barriers
  • Professional licensing restrictions
  • Housing barriers
  • Volunteer opportunities
  • Firearm rights restoration

Supporting documentation should be obtained whenever possible.

10. Personal Narrative

The personal statement should address:

  • What happened.
  • Acceptance of responsibility.
  • What has been learned.
  • Evidence of rehabilitation.
  • Why clemency is appropriate now.

Paper Chasers Clemency Intake Checklist

Required:

  • Photo identification
  • Address verification
  • Case numbers
  • Judgments of conviction
  • Sentence discharge records
  • Employment history
  • Education history
  • Character references
  • Personal statement

Strongly Recommended:

  • Treatment records
  • Volunteer documentation
  • Professional licenses
  • Awards and certificates
  • Employer recommendation letters
  • Community support letters

Optional but Helpful:

  • Military records
  • Business ownership documents
  • Newspaper articles
  • Civic involvement records
  • Documentation showing specific need for clemency
