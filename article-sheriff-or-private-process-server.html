Sheriff or Private Process Server? What Fox Valley Landlords Should Know

By Michael Winius, Paper Chasers LLC
Disclaimer: This article is provided for educational and informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Paper Chasers LLC is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice or legal representation. Court rules and local practices change, and every case is different. Confirm current requirements with the clerk of courts, and speak with a qualified attorney about your specific situation.

Introduction

In Wisconsin, eviction papers can be served by the county sheriff or by any adult who is not a party to the case (Wis. Stat. § 801.10). Both are legitimate choices. The right one depends on your timeline, your tenant and how much detail you want in the proof of service.

The County Sheriff

Sheriff's offices in Outagamie, Winnebago, Calumet and Brown counties serve civil process, including eviction papers. Deputies are trained and familiar with the courts, and their fees are set by the county. The trade-off is that civil process is one of many duties, so the number and timing of attempts depend on the office's workload. Check your county sheriff's website for current fees and instructions.

A Private Process Server

A private server works for you. That usually means more flexibility:

  • Timing: attempts early in the morning, in the evening and on weekends, when tenants are most likely to be home.
  • Persistence: more attempts when a tenant is avoiding service.
  • Communication: updates after each attempt, so you know where things stand before your court date.
  • Documentation: a detailed proof of service listing every attempt, which matters if service is challenged.
  • One contact: the same server can also file papers or deliver documents to the courthouse.

Questions to Ask Any Server

  • How many attempts are included, and at what times of day?
  • How soon will the first attempt be made?
  • Will I get an update after each attempt?
  • What does the proof of service include?
  • What areas do you cover, and is there a travel charge?
  • Is there a rush option when the court date is close?

When a Private Server Makes the Most Sense

  • The return date is close and there is little room for failed attempts.
  • The tenant works nights or is likely to avoid the door.
  • You manage several units and want one consistent process.
  • You want detailed records of every attempt in your file.
